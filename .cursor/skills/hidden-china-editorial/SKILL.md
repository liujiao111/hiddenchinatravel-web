# Hidden China Editorial

## Purpose

Produce English content that sounds like a careful local travel partner: specific, calm, practical, and willing to make a real judgment. This skill is inspired by Humanizer Pro's audit-first, restraint-first approach, but is tailored to Hidden China Travel.

It does **not** try to evade AI detection. It does **not** fabricate personality, travel experience, current facts, reviews, quotes, or local recommendations.

## Read first

1. Read `webiste_info.md`. It is the project’s governing content, brand, URL, affiliate, and internal-link baseline.
2. For Dali work, also read `docs/yunnan/dali/seo-content-and-conversion-playbook.md`.
3. Read the target article and its related existing hub/article pages before planning new links.
4. If the assignment depends on current operations—transport, tickets, opening hours, visa policy, prices, regulations, or availability—verify against a primary/official source before stating it as fact. If it cannot be verified, remove false precision or label it as a traveler/author observation.

## Operating modes

Choose the least invasive mode that answers the request.

### 1. Write

Use when drafting a new article or substantial section.

1. Identify the search intent and the reader’s decision.
2. Ask for firsthand knowledge only if it materially improves the article: places the author knows, what they would choose or skip, traveler mistakes, honest trade-offs, photos, and intended audience.
3. Place those real inputs at the centre of the draft. SEO structure supports the insight; it does not replace it.
4. Answer the reader’s practical question in the opening 100–160 words.
5. Use headings only where they help a traveler make the next decision. Do not manufacture a symmetrical 10-item structure.
6. Add only verified facts and relevant internal links.
7. Do a silent editorial audit before handing over the draft.

### 2. Review (default for an existing draft)

Use when asked whether copy is good, natural, useful, or ready to publish. Do not change the text unless asked.

Return:

1. **Outcome:** ready, nearly ready, or not ready.
2. **Blockers:** factual, trust, conversion, or search-intent problems that must change.
3. **Evidence:** short quoted examples or precise section references.
4. **Optional refinements:** only after blockers, and clearly separated.

Never treat a style score or a pattern flag as proof that a person used AI.

### 3. Minimal edit

Use when asked to make prose less AI-like, more natural, clearer, tighter, or more in Joy's voice.

1. Preserve correct, specific, human-written sentences.
2. Change the smallest possible unit: a word, sentence, paragraph, or heading.
3. Preserve facts, links, numbers, proper nouns, dates, prices, and author judgments unless correcting a verified error.
4. Do not swap formal language for fake casualness, invented anecdotes, slang, fragments, or exaggerated opinion.
5. Report only material changes and any remaining factual uncertainty.

### 4. Full editorial audit

Use only when asked for a full rewrite or full audit.

Audit in this order:

1. factual and source risk;
2. intent and usefulness;
3. author voice and lived knowledge;
4. SEO overlap and internal links;
5. conversion integrity;
6. AI-pattern / style cleanup;
7. fidelity check after revisions.

## Voice

Write for foreign visitors travelling China independently. Assume they may not speak Chinese, have no Chinese phone number or bank card, and do not understand local apps or booking systems.

The voice is:

- practical, direct, current, and honest;
- calm rather than promotional;
- specific about friction, trade-offs, and what to skip;
- confident only where evidence or lived experience supports it;
- first-person (`I`, Joy) only for genuine author experience or judgment;
- `we` only for the service offering.

Prefer:

- “Stay in the old town if convenience matters more than a lake view.”
- “A whole-lake circuit is not necessary for a good Erhai day.”
- “Check the station name on the ticket before booking a hotel nearby.”

Avoid:

- tourism-board praise: “breathtaking,” “magical,” “vibrant,” “ultimate,” “unforgettable”;
- abstract claims: “a testament to,” “an iconic destination,” “a must-visit”;
- false balance when a useful recommendation is available;
- generic conclusions that repeat the opening;
- artificial informality such as “Here’s the thing” or invented hesitation;
- treating any city as a checklist, tour product, or generic listicle.

Use simple verbs: `is`, `has`, `shows`, `choose`, `skip`, `check`, `book`, `walk`. Do not inflate them into `serves as`, `boasts`, `showcases`, or `underscores`.

## SEO and information architecture

- One article owns one primary search intent. Do not create a new URL solely for a modifier with the same intent.
- Give the answer early, then earn search coverage through useful detail.
- Use the exact primary query naturally in the title/H1, introduction, and metadata. Do not repeat it mechanically.
- Use a descriptive, clean root-level article slug unless existing architecture requires otherwise.
- Add FAQs only for actual unanswered user questions; FAQ schema does not guarantee a search result feature.
- Use relative links only. Never invent a URL; distinguish planned from published pages.
- Link articles to the reader’s real next step, not to every page in the topic cluster.
- All article links open in a new tab, per the project baseline.

## Conversion integrity

Guide readers according to their decision stage:

| Reader need | Default action |
| --- | --- |
| Needs basic China setup | Survival Kit / practical system guide |
| Is choosing cities, days, pace, or overnight base | Itinerary planner |
| Is ready to book a train, flight, hotel, or ticket | Relevant affiliate execution link, only if it is truly useful |

Rules:

- Do not turn every section into a sales pitch.
- The planner belongs where the route itself is unresolved—not after a simple payment, map, or train instruction.
- Explain the paid service accurately: a one-to-one, independent-travel planning PDF and local-partner advice; not a guided package or bus tour.
- Paid itinerary planning remains fee-only. Do not claim it has no affiliate links; instead say paid planning does not take hotel, attraction, or OTA commissions.
- Affiliate links use `/go/...`, must be relevant, and need the project’s approved disclosure.
- Do not show an incorrect price tier. Verify the route’s trip-length pricing before using a price in CTA copy.
- Prefer one context-specific CTA and one end-of-article CTA over repeated generic prompts.

## Required final checks

Before marking content ready:

- Does the introduction answer the query?
- Does every concrete claim have a source or honest author basis?
- Did we preserve Joy’s actual observation rather than simulating a voice?
- Did we remove generic praise, filler, forced contrasts, and tidy-but-empty summaries?
- Do headings reflect decisions a traveler needs to make?
- Are links real, relative, and selective?
- Are affiliate disclosures and service claims accurate?
- Does the CTA match the reader’s intent and price tier?
- Does the copy remain useful even if the reader never buys anything?

For the full tell catalogue and destination-specific checks, use `references/editorial-audit.md`.
